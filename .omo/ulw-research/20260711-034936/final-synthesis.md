# Final Research Digest

## Answer in one page

The AI data market evolved from labor and static labels into expert demonstrations, preferences, hard tasks, evaluations, verifiers, and stateful environments. The newest “RL environment” suppliers still combine software with expert recruiting, program management, review, and forward-deployed delivery. They usually sell through narrow proofs and expand after acceptance; they do not behave like pure self-serve SaaS.

The ten named-company dossiers divide into three broad groups:

- Scale, Mercor, Micro1, Turing, and AfterQuery combine expert supply/data with evaluation and environment offerings.
- Fleet, Deeptune, Refresh, Vibrant, and Halluminate are smaller specialists emphasizing environment engineering, hard-task discovery, or a vertical expert network.
- Adjacent platforms, runtime providers, assurance firms, and open standards increasingly commoditize the horizontal layers.

Headline metrics require normalization. Scale's Meta deal is a minority investment, not an acquisition. Mercor's run rate is gross marketplace volume with large contractor pass-through. Turing's $300M/profitability statement is company-reported. AfterQuery's Series A is confirmed but its $100M run rate and every-lab language are unaudited; NVIDIA independently confirms one important use. Fleet's financing/revenue reports are not confirmed. Deeptune's Mercor transaction is announced, with price and legal close unknown.

The entrant thesis changed under countersearch. LangSmith already owns much of the generic trace-to-improvement workflow; Browserbase/Prime/OpenEnv reduce runtime differentiation; AWS AgentCore bundles horizontal agent infrastructure; and PwC, Deloitte, and BSI already occupy formal assurance. Generic enterprise willingness-to-pay for independent evaluation is not proven.

The remaining entrant candidate is a narrow Domain Assurance Pack for a buyer facing an external-evidence decision. It is a pre-score research hypothesis, not a selected recommendation, and must clear the website's scorecard and no-go gate. If it clears, run it in the buyer's environment, use rights-cleared private tasks and state, expert-calibrated verifiers, hidden holdouts, and validity attacks, then export a signed technical release/procurement report and interoperable artifacts. Do not claim certification without authority. Partner with formal assurance and cloud channels.

## Highest-confidence findings

1. The market's purchased unit has shifted from rows to versioned worlds, tasks, trajectories, and verifiers, while older layers remain.
2. RLHF/RLAIF describe feedback provenance; DPO is an objective; RLVR describes checkable reward. They are not peer company categories.
3. Environment runtime is standardizing; tasks, state, verifiers, expert adjudication, rights, and freshness are more defensible.
4. The dominant GTM is technical relationship → paid pilot → acceptance proof → forward-deployed expansion.
5. Gross marketplace run rate, net revenue, and SaaS ARR are not comparable.
6. Independent evaluation demand is strongest where an external decision or safety/public accountability exists.
7. A data flywheel exists only when contracts permit retention and reuse.
